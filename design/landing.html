Loading it all would need 10 GB, more than the laptop has, so it would crash or start swapping. So I read a chunk, say 8 KB, into one buffer, count it, and reuse the same buffer for the next chunk. Memory stays about 8 KB whether the file is 1 MB or 10 GB.

How it works

One project, four moves.

  1. 01 · about 2 minutes

    Read the problem in plain words

    Every project opens with a short primer: what you're building, why it exists, a tiny worked example, and the terms you'll meet.

  2. 02 · 10 to 20 minutes a step

    Answer each step

    Each step asks how you'd approach a piece of the problem, or what a bit of code will do. It's done when your reasoning is right, not when your code compiles.

  3. 03 · once per milestone

    Explain it back, out loud

    Talk the milestone through. Each idea you cover lights up, and your own words are kept as proof you understood it.

  4. 04 · always yours

    Keep what you learned

    Ideas you've explained stay explained in every project that uses them. Export your learning log any time.

The mentor

Help when you ask for it. Never before.

The mentor is an AI agent. It reads your answers, the project's notes and what you've already learned, then decides how to help: a question, a nudge, a small example. You choose how far it goes.

  1. Hinta nudge in words
  2. Similar casenot your code
  3. Ask the mentortalk it through
  4. Show me codeonly when you ask
least helpmost help

Anything beyond a hint is noted in your help log. You still explain the idea back yourself.

It will

  • Explain with words, analogies and small cases
  • Give whatever level of help you ask for, without making you prove you tried
  • Offer more help when you're stuck, and take no for an answer

It won't

  • Write your solution unless you ask for it
  • Change your code. It can only read your repo
  • Mark anything done. Only the checks can

Questions

Before you start

Do I need to know a programming language?

No. You can work through the ideas only. Pick a language when you want to build it, and you can link a public GitHub repo for ThinkAloud to read.

Will it write my code?

Only if you ask, from More help, in short explained parts. It never changes your code.

Who checks my answers?

Answers that are a number or a short fact are checked against a stored key. Explanations are graded by AI against a fixed list of points, and every point it marks as covered quotes your own words. If a grade looks wrong, you can flag it.

Do I need Wispr Flow?

No. Everything works with typing. Flow makes long explanations quicker to give, and it has a free plan.

What do I end up with?

A learning log: every idea you've explained, in your own words, with the help you used. Export it as Markdown or JSON and keep it, share it, or put it next to your project on GitHub.

Is it free?

Yes, and there's no sign-up. Your progress stays in your browser unless you choose to save it.
